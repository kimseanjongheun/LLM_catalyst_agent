Introduction

The design of bimetallic catalysts for hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) presents a significant challenge due to the vast search space and the computational cost associated with density functional theory (DFT) calculations. Traditional approaches to catalyst discovery rely heavily on trial-and-error methods or exhaustive computational screening, which are both time-consuming and resource-intensive. Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have opened new possibilities for automated hypothesis generation and intelligent candidate selection in materials science. This study presents a proof-of-concept implementation that investigates the feasibility of LLM agent-based material screening for bimetallic catalyst discovery.

The field of computational materials science has witnessed a paradigm shift with the integration of machine learning potentials (MLPs) such as Universal Neural Network Interatomic Potential (UMA), which have significantly reduced the computational bottleneck in molecular dynamics simulations. This advancement, combined with the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, enables a novel approach to materials discovery through iterative hypothesis generation and validation cycles. The proposed workflow leverages LLMs for intelligent hypothesis generation, candidate selection, and iterative improvement through a reasoning-simulation feedback loop, potentially revolutionizing the way we approach catalyst design.

This work makes several key contributions to the field of computational materials discovery: (i) the development of a comprehensive workflow that integrates LLM-based reasoning with high-throughput simulation capabilities, (ii) the establishment of quantitative metrics for evaluating success rates, reasoning quality, and computational cost efficiency, (iii) a systematic comparison demonstrating the superiority of LLM-guided selection over random sampling approaches, and (iv) a comparative analysis of different model architectures to identify optimal configurations for materials discovery tasks.
