가설 수정 결과: Updated hypothesis (specific, testable)

When alloying a strong H-binding metal (Pt, Pd, Ni, Ru, Rh, Ir) with a weaker H-binding partner (Au, Ag, Cu, Zn, Cd, Hg) at surface-enriching compositions (0.75:0.25 or 0.50:0.50), ligand and ensemble effects will in general tune ΔE_H toward the optimal −0.27 eV — but with metal-specific offsets and non-monotonic behavior that must be accounted for. Concretely:

1. Pt-based alloys with late noble weak-binders (Au, Ag) reliably produce ΔE_H values close to −0.27 eV at 0.75:0.25 (top sites gave −0.2787 and −0.2799 eV for Pt0.75–Au0.25 and Pt0.75–Ag0.25), so Pt:Au and Pt:Ag remain high-priority families.
2. Pd-based alloys can deviate substantially from Pt trends: Pd0.75–Au0.25 (top) produced an unexpectedly weak (positive) ΔE_H (+0.3551 eV), so Pd–Au alloys may over-weaken H binding at top sites (surface segregation/ligand shift stronger than predicted). Thus Pd alloys require expanded composition/site sampling (including 0.50:0.50 and more-coordinated sites) before being classified as promising.
3. The hollow > bridge > top site binding trend remains valid in most systems, but metal-specific electronic/segregation effects can invert or compress these site-dependent differences for some alloys (evidence: Pd0.75–Au0.25 top is much weaker than anticipated). Therefore site sampling remains essential, but we must expect exceptions.
4. Composition dependence is generally monotonic for Pt-based pairs (0.75→0.50 should move ΔE_H toward the weak-binder endpoint), but Pd-containing and some other families may show non-monotonic or abrupt shifts due to surface segregation or strong ligand shifts; hence both 0.75:0.25 and 0.50:0.50 must be tested for these metals.
5. Therefore the highest density of near-optimal catalysts will still likely be among Pt–(Au,Ag,Cu) and similar Pt–weak pairs at 0.75:0.25 (top/bridge), but Pd-containing systems require extra caution — sample both compositions and more coordinated sites to capture possible strong weakening.
