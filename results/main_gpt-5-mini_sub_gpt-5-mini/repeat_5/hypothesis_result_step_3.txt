When alloying a strong H-binding metal (Pt, Pd, Ni, Ru, Rh, Ir) with a weaker H-binding partner (Au, Ag, Cu, Zn, Cd, Hg) at surface-enriched compositions, ligand/ensemble effects generally tune ΔE_H toward the optimal −0.27 eV — but metal- and site-specific offsets (and sometimes abrupt deviations) are important. The new data refine that general statement as follows:

1) Pt family (confirmed)
- Pt–Au and Pt–Ag at 0.75:0.25 (top) reproducibly give ΔE_H ≈ −0.28 eV. Hypothesis: for Pt0.75–X0.25 (X = Au, Ag) the top and bridge sites will remain within ±0.10 eV of −0.27; hollow will be more negative by ~0.05–0.15 eV.
- Testable prediction: Pt0.75–Cu0.25 (top) will bind H stronger than Pt0.75–Au0.25(top) by ~0.05–0.20 eV (i.e., ΔE_H ≈ −0.35 to −0.23 eV); bridge/hollow will be stronger than top.

2) Pd–Au is an anomalous outlier
- Observation: Pd0.75–Au0.25 (top) = +0.3551 eV (large, unexpected weakening).
- Revised hypothesis: the Pd–Au pair shows an Au-driven upward shift in ΔE_H at Pd0.75–Au0.25(top) that is larger than for other strong/weak pairs. This shift is likely driven by either (a) Au surface segregation/ensemble formation that destabilizes H adsorption on Pd top sites, or (b) a Pd-specific electronic interaction with Au that raises the H adsorption energy.
- Testable predictions:
  - For Pd0.75–Au0.25, bridge and hollow sites will be measurably more negative (stronger-binding) than the anomalous top value, but they may still be substantially weaker than −0.27 (i.e., bridge/hollow could lie anywhere from ≈ +0.10 to −0.30 eV).
  - Pd0.50–Au0.50 (if available) will move ΔE_H more negative than +0.3551 by at least ~0.15–0.30 eV if the anomaly is due to surface Au enrichment at 0.75:0.25; if not, Pd–Au may be anomalous across compositions.
  - Pd0.75–Ag0.25 will not reproduce the full Pd–Au anomaly: expect Pd0.75–Ag0.25(top) to be more negative than Pd0.75–Au0.25(top) by ≳0.20 eV.

3) Site and composition sampling remain essential, but emphasis differs by metal family
- For Pt-family alloys, coarser sampling (0.75:0.25 top/bridge ± select 0.50:0.50) is generally sufficient to find near-optimal ΔE_H.
- For Pd-containing alloys, dense sampling across sites (top/bridge/hollow) and compositions (0.75:0.25 and 0.50:0.50 where available) is required because of potential Au-specific or composition-dependent anomalies.
