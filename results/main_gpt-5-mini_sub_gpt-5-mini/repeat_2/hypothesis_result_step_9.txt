- Noble–non‑noble bimetallics can reproducibly reach near‑optimal H adsorption energies (ΔE_H ≈ −0.27 eV), but only inside narrow, pair‑specific windows defined by composition × adsorption site × ordering × surface‑layer exposure.

Hypothesis (modified with simulation evidence)
- The core claim stands but must be tightened and partially relaxed: near‑optimal ΔE_H ≈ −0.27 eV is attainable in noble–non‑noble bimetallics, but the windows are extremely narrow and highly configuration‑dependent. Composition (Δcomposition ≈ 0.25), adsorption site (hollow vs bridge vs top), and surface ordering (which element is surface‑exposed) each produce shifts on the order of 0.1–1.0 eV; typical magnitudes observed:
  - Hollow→bridge/top typically weakens binding by ~0.1–0.2 eV (example: Pd–Ni 75:25 hollow −0.2528 eV vs bridge −0.0559 eV; Ni–Pd 75:25 hollow −0.2474 eV vs bridge −0.1012 eV).
  - Reversing ordering (which element is exposed) can produce modest shifts ~0.1–0.2 eV in many cases but can also produce very large changes if an early TM becomes surface‑exposed (examples below).
  - Exposing early transition metals on the surface often causes extreme overbinding (up to ≈ −1.0 eV): V‑exposed V–Pt 75:25 hollow = −0.9927 eV; other strong overbinders include Ag–Re 50:50 bridge = −0.6132 eV and Ag–Mo 50:50 hollow = −0.6029 eV.
  - Composition tuning by ≈0.25 fraction can shift ΔE_H by ≈0.25 eV (Au–W: 75:25 top −0.3924 eV vs 50:50 top −0.1419 eV → Δ ≈ 0.2505 eV).
  - Near‑optimal examples observed in current dataset (hollow site unless noted): Pd–Ni 75:25 (Pd‑exposed hollow) −0.2528 eV; Ni–Pd 75:25 (Ni‑exposed hollow) −0.2474 eV; Ir–Fe 75:25 (Ir‑exposed hollow) −0.2624 eV; Ir–Ni 50:50 hollow −0.2756 eV (shows a 50:50 can also hit the sweet spot).
  - Many Pd/Pt 75:25 hollow combinations cluster at weaker binding than −0.27 eV (examples: Pt–Ni 75:25 hollow −0.1822 eV; Pd–Co 75:25 hollow −0.1903 eV), while some Ru/Rh/early‑TM partners overbind (Rh–Ni −0.3461 eV; Ru–Fe −0.4450 eV).
  - Strong site/order extremes: Pt–V 75:25 top (Pt‑exposed top) = +0.3617 eV (severe underbinding), whereas V–Pt 75:25 hollow (V‑exposed hollow) = −0.9927 eV (severe overbinding). Note Pt–V with Pt exposed at hollow is intermediate (Pt‑V 75:25 hollow = −0.1188 eV), illustrating that ordering + site together control sign and magnitude.
- Practical corollaries from the data:
  - The “sweet spot” is pair‑specific and often (but not always) occurs at noble‑rich 75:25 on hollow sites (Pd–Ni, Ir–Fe), yet equally good ΔE_H can occur at other compositions (Ir–Ni 50:50). There is no universal composition/site that guarantees −0.27 eV across different pairs.
  - Scanning composition in ~0.25 increments and the three adsorption sites is an effective coarse search strategy; it will typically reveal at most a single narrow composition×site window that approaches −0.27 eV for any given pair.
  - Database coverage matters: several requested configurations were not present in the dataset (the "조건을 만족하는 후보가 없습니다" entries); conclusions must be restricted to available combinations.

Updated testable predictions (refinements)
1. For a given metal pair available in the database, coarse scans at 0.25 composition intervals across top/bridge/hollow will locate at most one or a very small number of windows (width ≲ 0.05 eV) near ΔE_H ≈ −0.27 eV.
2. Noble‑rich 75:25 hollow windows are common for Pd–Ni and Ir–Fe, but other pairs will require different composition or ordering (e.g., Ir–Ni achieves the target at 50:50 hollow).
3. Switching from hollow → bridge/top or reversing which metal is surface‑exposed will most often shift ΔE_H toward weaker binding by O(0.1–0.2 eV), but exposing early TMs can produce large overbinding (≈ −0.6 to −1.0 eV).
4. Composition changes of ≈0.25 can move ΔE_H by ≈0.25 eV and thus can both create and destroy the sweet spot (as in Au–W).
5. Because site and ordering effects can be larger than composition increments in some pairs, exhaustive identification of the sweet spot requires testing both orderings (Metal1/Metal2) and all three adsorption sites.

Concise revised hypothesis statement
- Near‑optimal ΔE_H ≈ −0.27 eV is achievable in noble–non‑noble bimetallics, but only inside very narrow, pair‑ and configuration‑specific windows. Noble‑rich 75:25 hollow is a recurring sweet spot (e.g., Pd–Ni, Ir–Fe), though some pairs (e.g., Ir–Ni) hit the target at other compositions (50:50). Adsorption site and surface ordering are decisive: changing hollow→bridge/top or flipping the exposed element typically weakens binding by ~0.1–0.2 eV, while exposing early TMs can cause extreme overbinding (≈ −0.6 to −1.0 eV). Composition steps of ≈0.25 shift ΔE_H by ≈0.25 eV and can therefore recover or spoil activity.
