Revised primary hypothesis
- In the current DB, alloys with a strong/moderately-strong H binder at ~0.75 surface fraction (Pt, Ru, Ir) alloyed with a late weak binder (Au, Ag, Cu) sampled at bridge sites reliably produce H adsorption energies near −0.27 ± 0.05 eV. Pd-containing alloys systematically deviate toward much weaker H binding in the tested configurations (both bridge and top), and therefore achieving −0.27 eV with Pd will require different partners, higher Pd surface fraction, or different adsorption sites (e.g., hollow) — do not assume Pt/Ru transferability to Pd. Transferability to other late strong binders (Rh, Ni) is selective and must be checked case-by-case.

Interpretation / knowledge accumulated
- Robust family rule extended: Pt0.75 + (Au/Ag/Cu) and Ru0.75–Cu0.25 bridge families remain validated. Ir0.75–Ag0.25 bridge now joins this validated set — implies the recipe extends beyond Pt/Ru to at least Ir when paired with Ag.
- Transferability is selective: Rh0.75 and Ni0.75 with Cu/Ag/Au do not automatically reproduce Pt/Ru performance (Rh–Cu, Ir–Cu, Ni–Cu are too weak), so the partner identity matters beyond just “late weak metal.”
- Pd is a clear outlier: across bridge and top sites (and across Au/Ag/Cu partners at 0.75 fraction) Pd alloys give much weaker adsorption — likely causes: (i) electronic d‑band suppression upon alloying, (ii) surface segregation / ensemble dilution of Pd active sites, or (iii) geometry/relaxation effects removing intended bridge ensembles. Pd behavior now appears systematically unfavorable for the −0.27 target in the tested entries.
- Site trend holds qualitatively (top weaker, hollow stronger) but alloy-specific electronic and ensemble effects can dominate.
