How these results support / contradict the original hypothesis
- Supported:
  - The core idea that Pt/Pd-rich alloys can be tuned toward −0.27 eV is partially supported: two tested Pt/Pd-rich entries (Pt–W top and Pd–W top) already fall within ±0.1 eV.
  - Site sensitivity is evident: top vs bridge (and likely hollow) matters strongly — Pt–V top was extremely weak, while bridge site data for Mo/Cr are substantially less negative than target.
- Contradicted / needs revision:
  - The prediction that Pd-rich alloys would be systematically weaker-binding (ΔEads less negative by ~0.05–0.15 eV vs Pt-rich analogs) is not supported for W: Pd–W(top) (−0.3276) binds slightly stronger than Pt–W(top) (−0.2933) by ≈0.034 eV (opposite sign). The dataset is small so no universal Pt vs Pd rule should be assumed.
  - Early metals Mo and Cr (and V at least in some sites) weaken H binding substantially more than was expected at Pt/Pd-rich composition and, for these tested sites, mostly produce values far from −0.27 eV (Δ > +0.16 eV). Thus the expectation that 25% early-metal dilution would routinely tune Pt/Pd to within ±0.1 eV is overly optimistic for many early metals.
  - Bridge-site behavior in tested entries (Mo/Cr bridges) yielded ΔEads much less negative than −0.27, so the simple ordering “hollow > bridge > top” as a universal lever should be treated as a trend, not a strict rule for all metal pairs.

Refined hypothesis (concise, testable)
- Within the provided database, Pt- and Pd-rich alloys can reach near-optimal H adsorption energies, but the effectiveness is strongly element-specific: W as Metal2 is especially promising at Pt/Pd-rich top sites (both Pt–W and Pd–W (0.75:0.25) top sites already approach −0.27 eV), whereas Mo, Cr and V (in the tested site/composition combinations) tend to over-weaken binding and are less likely to hit −0.27 eV at 0.75:0.25 unless site or composition are changed. Pd does not reliably give weaker binding than Pt; sign and magnitude of the Pt vs Pd shift depend on Metal2 and site.
