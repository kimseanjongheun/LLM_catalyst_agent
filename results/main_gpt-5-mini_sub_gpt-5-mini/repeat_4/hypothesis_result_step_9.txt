B. Refined, testable hypothesis (concise)
- Core hypothesis (refined): W-containing bimetallics still offer the most direct path to the −0.27 eV HER adsorption target, but the adsorption energy is a strongly non-linear function of (i) noble-metal identity (Pt vs Pd vs Cu/Au), (ii) composition (discrete steps produce large changes), and (iii) adsorption site. Avoid bridge sites near equimolar W (they over-bind). Hollow sites can either mitigate or overshoot depending on the noble metal (Pd-hollow mitigates; Pt-hollow often over-weakens). Re is a reliable knob to increase top-site binding (risk of over-binding), whereas V, Nb, Hf strongly weaken adsorption (risk of too-weak adsorption).
- Specific, testable predictions:
  1. Small increases in Pt fraction from an already Pt-rich Pt–W (e.g., from 0.75 → somewhat higher Pt fraction) will only modestly strengthen top-site binding; however the DB discretization is coarse (0.75 vs 0.50), so measured changes across available fractions will be non-linear and potentially large when crossing fraction bins (0.75→0.50 showed large weakening already).
  2. Pd–W remains more prone to over-binding than Pt–W at the same discretized fraction (evidence: Pd–W–0.75–top is more negative than Pt–W–0.75–top).
  3. Re addition at significant fractions (0.25+ Re) will shift top-site adsorption ~0.09–0.12 eV more negative (based on Pt–Re and Pd–Re 0.75 results); test lower-Re fractions to find mitigation range (0.50 or less Re fraction expected to be less over-binding).
  4. Pt–Zn at bridge site is already near-target; top site for Pt–Zn will likely be similarly near-target (±0.03 eV); Zn is a promising diluent to explore as an alternative to W for fine tuning.
  5. Cu–W and Pd–W hollow configurations at 0.50 composition are experimentally promising (already near-target); expand hollow-site scans for Cu and Pd with W at 0.50 composition.
  6. Avoid V/Hf/Nb dopants if the goal is to reach −0.27 eV (they move too far weak); use them only if intentionally searching for weak-binding catalysts.
