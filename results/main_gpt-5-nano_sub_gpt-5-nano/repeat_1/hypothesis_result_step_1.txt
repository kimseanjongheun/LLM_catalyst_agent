Hypothesis

There is a distinct, under-explored region of the database where hollow adsorption on certain bimetallic alloys yields hydrogen adsorption energies (ΔG_H*) closest to the optimal value of –0.27 eV for HER. This optimal window is most likely to be realized when:

- Metal1_Composition is 0.75 with Metal2_Composition 0.25, or Metal1_Composition is 0.50 with Metal2_Composition 0.50.
- Metal1 is a Pt-group metal (Pt, Ir, Pd, Rh, Ru) to provide a strong, tunable d-band foundation, while Metal2 is a second metal that modulates the d-band and local ensemble effects (Cu, Ni, Co, Fe, Zn, Nb, Ti, V, etc.).
- The adsorption site geometry is hollow, because the three-atom ensemble at hollow sites affords cooperative bonding that better tunes the d-band center and H bonding than single-atom (top) or two-atom (bridge) sites.

Predictions and rationale

- Hollow site performance: Across many Pt-group Metal1 / various Metal2 combinations, ΔG_H* at hollow sites will cluster around –0.27 eV with a higher hit rate than top or bridge sites, due to synergistic multi-metal bonding that effectively tunes the local electronic structure at the adsorption triangle.
- Composition effect: For Metal1_Composition = 0.75 (0.25 Metal2), the target window will be accessible for a larger portion of Metal2 choices than for the 0.50/0.50 case, because the dominant Metal1 motif sets a robust d-band environment that is modulated by a minority second metal. The 0.50/0.50 domain should still yield many near-optimal candidates but with more dispersion.
- Metal1 metal identity: Pt-group Metal1 combinations will more reliably approach the –0.27 eV goal in hollow sites than non-Pt-group Metal1 combinations, due to a balance of strong base binding and tuneability, though there will be notable non-noble-metal outliers.
- Unexpected winners: Some non-noble-metal combinations (e.g., Nb/Fe, Ti/Ni, Zr/Co) in hollow sites may also approach or reach ΔG_H* ≈ –0.27 eV, offering potentially lower-cost catalysts.

- Prioritized axis for the hypothesis:
  - Primary axis: hollow site candidates with Metal1_Composition 0.75 (Metal2 0.25) and Metal1 in Pt-group (Pt, Ir, Pd, Rh, Ru)
  - Secondary axis: hollow site candidates with Metal1_Composition 0.50 (Metal2 0.50), across Pt-group and a subset of non-Pt metals
  - Tertiary axis: select a small set of non-Pt Metal1 pairs (e.g., Nb, Ti, Zr, V) with various Metal2 choices, focusing on hollow sites

- Iteration refinement: If hollow-site candidates rarely hit –0.27 eV, reassess whether the assumption about Pt-group Metal1 driving the window holds and consider expanding Metal1 to include adjacent d-band centers (while still respecting the 0.75/0.25 or 0.50/0.50 constraints).
- Decision rules for next iteration: Prioritize new hollow-site combinations with ΔG_H* within ±0.05 eV of –0.27 eV; if insufficient hits, broaden Metal2 choices (within allowed database) or test the 0.50/0.50 domain with additional Pt-group Metal1s.

Scientific rationale you can reference in discussion

- d-band tuning and ensemble effects: Bimettallic ensembles modify the local d-band center and the bonding environment for H. Hollow sites involve three metal atoms, enabling cooperative interactions that often move ΔG_H* toward zero more effectively than isolated atop sites.
- Composition strategies: The 0.75/0.25 motif tends to preserve the strong, favorable characteristics of a Pt-group primary metal while allowing the minority second metal to fine-tune adsorption energetics. The 0.50/0.50 domain tests whether dual-midpoint enrichment broadens the accessible ΔG_H* window without sacrificing too much stability.
- Practical relevance: If hollow-site, Pt-group–dominated alloys consistently reach ΔG_H* ≈ –0.27 eV, these candidates emerge as high-priority targets for further DFT refinement and experimental validation, including potential cost-focused non-Pt outliers.
