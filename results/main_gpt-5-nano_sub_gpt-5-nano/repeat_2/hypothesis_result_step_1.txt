Hypothesis

In the pre-DFT screening database of 4,641 bimetallic adsorption sites for HER, the hydrogen adsorption energy (H-E ads) that is closest to the optimal value of -0.27 eV will be most reliably achieved in hollow adsorption geometries on metal1-rich Pt- and Pd-based alloys when the second metal is a light-to-mid transition metal (Ni, Co, Fe, or Cu) and the composition is strongly skewed toward Metal1 (Metal1_Composition = 0.75, Metal2_Composition = 0.25). This combination will exploit (i) favorable d-band tuning from Pt/Pd with a secondary late transition metal, and (ii) the three-atom ensemble effects that stabilize H adsorption at hollow sites, leading to the smallest deviations from -0.27 eV across newly tested, non-duplicate candidates.

Specific, testable predictions

- Prediction 1 (hollow-site focus): Among entries with Metal1 in [Pt, Pd] and Metal2 in [Ni, Co, Fe, Cu], a subset with Metal1_Composition = 0.75, Metal2_Composition = 0.25, and Site = hollow will yield H-Eads values within ±0.05 eV of -0.27 eV in multiple unique, previously untested combinations.

- Prediction 2 (site comparison): For the same Pt/Pd–Ni/Co/Fe/Cu family, hollow sites will show closer-to-target energies than top or bridge sites; top and bridge energies will deviate by larger margins (e.g., >0.05–0.10 eV) from -0.27 eV under equivalent metal-pair conditions.

- Prediction 3 (composition sensitivity): In the 0.50/0.50 composition regime, Pt- and Pd-based alloys with the same secondary metals will exhibit a broader distribution of H-Eads with fewer members near -0.27 eV, compared with the 0.75/0.25 regime.

- Prediction 4 (metal identity effect): When Metal1 ∈ Pt or Pd and Metal2 ∈ Ni, Co, Fe, or Cu, there will be a stronger propensity for near -0.27 eV values at hollow sites than for other Metal1 classes (e.g., Ir/Ru or non-Pt/Pd noble/coinage metals) under similar compositions.

- Prediction 5 (electronic-structure rationale): A smaller difference in electronegativity and a d-band center shift toward the H 1s interaction (as inferred from known Pt/Pd–Ni/Co/Fe/Cu alloying trends) will correlate with H-Eads values closer to -0.27 eV across the hollow-site entries, providing a predictive link between alloy electronic properties and adsorption performance.

Rationale (why these predictions should hold)

- The optimal HER descriptor a = -0.27 eV for H adsorption is often realized when the d-band center of the active site is tuned to balance adsorption strength. Bimetallic Pt-/Pd-based systems with a light-to-mid transition metal second component can shift the d-band center toward this optimum.
- Hollow sites inherently involve three-atom ensembles, which enhance electronic and geometric ensemble effects that can stabilize H adsorption in a way that brings E(H) closer to the target value, especially when one metal dominates the surface (Metal1_Composition = 0.75).
- Composition skew toward Metal1 should preserve the favorable Pt/Pd-like surface character while enabling tuning by the secondary metal, increasing the likelihood of hitting the -0.27 eV target across candidates that have not yet been tested.
- Top and bridge sites may not harness the same ensemble effects to the same extent, leading to larger deviations from the optimum energy in many combinations.

Operational guidance for the 10 iterative loops (brief)

- Focus the search on non-duplicate entries within Pt- and Pd-based pairs with secondary metals Ni, Co, Fe, or Cu, using Metal1_Composition = 0.75 and Metal2_Composition = 0.25, and Site = hollow as the primary target. Expand to related pairs only after confirming the hollow-site trend.
- In each loop, catalog candidates that meet the prediction thresholds (e.g., within ±0.05 eV of -0.27 eV for hollow Pt/Pd–Ni/Co/Fe/Cu) and ensure they are novel (not previously tested).
- If hollow Pt/Pd-based combinations do not yield sufficient near-optimal entries, broaden to nearby second metals (e.g., Zn, Mn) or slightly adjust the composition toward 0.50/0.50, but with the expectation of a lower hit-rate near -0.27 eV.
- Record every new finding (ads energy, metal pair, site, composition, and any observed trends) to refine the correlation between metal identity, site geometry, and proximity to -0.27 eV, feeding back into hypothesis refinement.

This hypothesis provides a concrete, testable framework that directs the intern toward novel, non-duplicate Pt- and Pd-based bimetallic candidates with hollow adsorption sites and a metal1-rich composition, while offering clear expectations about site dependence, composition effects, and underlying electronic-structure rationale.
