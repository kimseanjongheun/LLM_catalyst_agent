가설 수정 결과: Revised hypothesis (v7) based on the latest simulation data

What held
- Pd + coinage rule strengthened: On Pd hosts with coinage partners (Cu, Ag, Au), non-hollow sites are severely destabilized; hollow is the only viable site and is often still weak (Pd–Ag is the best of the set).
- Pt with heavy coinage (Ag, Au): Site shifts are small and can be non-monotonic; top can slightly strengthen relative to hollow (Pt–Au case).
- 3d–Cu with 3d host at 0.75 (Ni–Cu, Co–Cu): Non-hollow sites induce very large weakening; these systems are not site-tunable near −0.27 at 0.75 3d fraction.
- Rh systems remain poorly tunable with current DB coverage: Rh–Cu hollow is slightly weak; Rh–Ag and Rh–Au hollow are too strong; absence of top/bridge prevents site rescue.

What changed (key corrections)
- Pt–Cu is a special case among “coinage on Pt”: Despite being coinage, Cu behaves unlike Ag/Au on Pt. Pt–Cu(0.75) shows extreme non-hollow weakening with top unbound and hollow far too weak:
  - Pt–Cu(0.75): hollow −0.1527; bridge −0.2334; top +0.7323 eV; Δh→t = +0.8850 eV.
  - Conclusion: Avoid Pt–Cu at 0.75; the “small-shift on Pt with coinage” rule applies to Ag/Au but not to Cu.
- Cu host with Co diluent gives a near-optimal hollow: Cu–Co(0.75) hollow = −0.2801 eV, confirming the “Cu-rich Co–Cu weakens H” trend and showing an immediately usable composition/domain on the Cu host side.
- Cu host with Ni diluent becomes too weak: Cu–Ni(0.75) hollow = −0.1902; Cu–Ni(0.50) hollow = −0.1212. Ni in Cu host monotonically weakens H with increasing Cu fraction (in the sampled range), making Cu-rich Ni–Cu unfavorable.
- Rh–Ni(0.75) hollow is too strong (−0.3461); no site lever present in DB to fix it.

Refined working model
- Site sensitivity is alloy- and ensemble-specific with a strong host/diluent asymmetry:
  - Pt hosts:
    - With Ag/Au diluents: site shifts are small and sometimes non-monotonic; top can slightly strengthen versus hollow.
    - With Cu or 3d diluents: non-hollow sites can massively weaken H (Pt–Cu top unbound; Pt–Ni top unbound); hollow often too weak.
  - Pd + coinage: Non-hollow sites access coinage-rich ensembles that severely destabilize H; hollow is the only usable site and is often too weak except Pd–Ag.
  - Cu hosts: Co as a diluent tunes hollow binding toward −0.27 in the Cu-rich regime (Cu0.75Co0.25 ≈ −0.28). Ni as a diluent drives hollow too weak in Cu-rich regimes.
  - 3d–Cu with 3d hosts: At 0.75 3d, hollow binds too strongly (≈−0.38) while non-hollow over-weakens; site switching cannot rescue; composition must flip to Cu host to approach target.
  - Rh systems: Hollow with coinage is too strong; with Cu slightly weak; with Ni too strong. DB lacks alternative sites to modulate adsorption.

Practical guidance (current best performers)
- Cu–Co(0.75) hollow: −0.2801 eV (adopt; closest to −0.27 on Cu-based).
- Pt–Ag(0.75) top: −0.2799 eV (adopt).
- Pt–Au(0.75) top: −0.2787 eV (adopt).
- Pt–Au(0.75) hollow: −0.2532 eV (use when slightly weaker binding is desired).
- Pd–Ag(0.75) hollow: −0.2911 eV (slightly strong but acceptable; avoid site switching).

Summary of the modification
- The “coinage on Pt” small-shift rule is narrowed to Ag/Au; Cu behaves like a strong non-hollow destabilizer on Pt (akin to 3d).
- A key positive result emerged: Cu-rich Co–Cu at hollow already achieves near-optimal binding (−0.2801), validating composition control on the Cu-host side as a practical path to −0.27.
- Ni–Cu on the Cu-host side is not promising in the Cu-rich regime (too weak); Rh systems remain constrained by DB site coverage.
