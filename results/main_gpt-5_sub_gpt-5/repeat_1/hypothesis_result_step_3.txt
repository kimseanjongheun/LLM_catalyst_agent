What held
- Site ordering still trends hollow > bridge > top in binding strength, but the magnitude of the site-induced shift is highly alloy- and ensemble-dependent.
- Coinage diluent effect on Pt hollow remains Cu > Au > Ag in weakening strength: Pt–Cu (too weak) > Pt–Au (slightly weak) > Pt–Ag (slightly strong).

What changed (key corrections)
- Site shifts are not uniform:
  - Modest for Pt–Ag/Au at 0.75: hollow→bridge weakens only by ~0.01–0.03 eV.
  - Very large for Pd–Ag and Ni–Cu (≥0.30 eV), indicating that bridge/top sites can sample coinage-rich ensembles that dramatically weaken H binding.
- Pd–Ag: the bridge site is dramatically too weak (+0.40 eV shift from hollow), invalidating the earlier assumption that bridge would gently tune hollow toward −0.27 eV for Pd hosts.
- Rh host: Ag at 25% strengthens relative to Cu as predicted, but overshoots to too strong at hollow (−0.3546 eV). Thus, for Rh, Cu is the better 25% diluent if hollow is the working site; Ag may require moving to weaker sites.

Refined working model
- Site sensitivity is alloy-specific: on Pt-based alloys the bridge shift is small (~0.02 eV), while on Pd–Ag and Ni–Cu it can exceed 0.3–0.4 eV due to coinage-rich bridge ensembles.
- Practical guidance:
  - Pt host: use Ag/Au at 25%. If hollow is −0.30 ± 0.02 (Pt–Ag), use top to approach −0.27. If hollow is −0.25 (Pt–Au), keep hollow.
  - Pd host: use Ag at 25% and keep hollow; avoid bridge/top.
  - Rh host: Cu at 25% is near/weak at hollow; Ag at 25% is too strong at hollow—bridge may rescue.
  - Ni/Co–Cu: expect large site-induced weakening; for Co–Cu, 0.75 bridge/top may be the only available tuning levers; Ni–Cu appears DB-limited.
