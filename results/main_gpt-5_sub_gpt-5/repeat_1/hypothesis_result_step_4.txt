가설 수정 결과: Revised hypothesis (v4) based on the latest simulation data

What held
- Site ordering persists: hollow binds strongest, then bridge, then top across all alloys tested.
- Pt hollow-site diluent order remains Cu (weakest) > Au > Ag (strongest).

What changed (key corrections)
- Pt–Ag 0.75: Top site successfully nudges binding from slightly strong to near-target. Total hollow→top weakening is small (+0.0226 eV), validating “gentle site tuning” on Pt hosts.
- Pd–Ag 0.75: Top is extremely weak (+0.2064 eV), confirming that non-hollow sites can sample Ag-rich ensembles with drastic weakening (hollow→top shift +0.4975 eV).
- Co–Cu 0.75: Site-induced weakening is enormous. Hollow is too strong (−0.3832), bridge too weak (−0.0342), and top unbound (+0.5649). This pair is not tunable by site at 0.75.
- Rh–Cu 0.75: Bridge weakens modestly from hollow (Δ +0.0935 eV), insufficient to reach −0.27. Site switching won’t rescue Rh–Cu at 0.75.
- Rh–Ag 0.75: Bridge unavailable in the DB; any rescue must come from the top site if present.

Updated, testable statements
- Pt host (0.75/0.25):
  - Pt–Ag: Top at −0.2799 eV is within 0.01 eV of the −0.27 target. Adopt top as the working site. Hollow (−0.3025) and bridge (−0.2887) are slightly strong but usable.
  - Pt–Au: Hollow (−0.2532) is acceptable; moving to bridge (−0.2265) is detrimental. Top likely weakens further; consider only for mapping.
  - Avoid Pt–Cu at 25% for optimization; hollow is far too weak (−0.1527).
- Pd host (0.75/0.25):
  - Pd–Ag: Use hollow (−0.2911). Bridge (0.1106) and top (0.2064) are much too weak; site switching is harmful.
  - Avoid Pd–Cu at 25% for optimization; hollow too weak (−0.1589).
- Rh host (0.75/0.25):
  - Rh–Cu: Hollow (−0.2303) slightly weak; bridge (−0.1368) weaker; top likely even weaker → not tunable by site.
  - Rh–Ag: Hollow too strong (−0.3546); bridge unavailable; test top if available to weaken toward −0.27.
- 3d–Cu systems:
  - Ni–Cu 0.75 and Co–Cu 0.75 exhibit very large site-induced weakening (≥0.35 eV, up to ~0.95 eV); they are not site-tunable around −0.27 within the present DB offerings.

Knowledge accumulated (new facts and patterns)
- Pt–Ag 0.75: hollow −0.3025; bridge −0.2887; top −0.2799 (Δh→b = +0.0138; Δb→t = +0.0088; Δh→t = +0.0226 eV).
- Pt–Au 0.75: hollow −0.2532; bridge −0.2265 (Δ = +0.0267 eV). Top not yet mapped.
- Pd–Ag 0.75: hollow −0.2911; bridge +0.1106; top +0.2064 (Δh→b = +0.4017; Δh→t = +0.4975; Δb→t = +0.0958 eV).
- Rh–Cu 0.75: hollow −0.2303; bridge −0.1368 (Δ = +0.0935 eV).
- Co–Cu 0.75: hollow −0.3832; bridge −0.0342; top +0.5649 (Δh→b = +0.349; Δb→t = +0.599; Δh→t = +0.948 eV).
- Reinforced trends:
  - Pt-based alloys exhibit small, quasi-linear site shifts (∼0.02–0.03 eV per step).
  - Pd–Ag and Co/Ni–Cu exhibit very large non-linear site shifts on non-hollow sites driven by coinage-rich bridge/top ensembles.
  - Rh–Cu shows moderate site sensitivity (~0.1 eV hollow→bridge).

Refined working model
- Alloy- and ensemble-specific site sensitivity:
  - Pt host: bridge/top induce gentle weakening; top can fine-tune when hollow is slightly strong (e.g., Pt–Ag).
  - Pd–Ag and 3d–Cu (Ni/Co–Cu): non-hollow sites can access coinage-rich ensembles, causing excessive weakening (even unbound H); hollow must be the working site if used at all.
  - Rh systems: modest site shifts; Rh–Cu cannot be tuned to −0.27 by site at 0.75; Rh–Ag requires a weaker site, but DB lacks bridge—top remains the only potential lever if available.
- Practical guidance:
  - Accept Pt–Ag 0.75 top (−0.2799), Pd–Ag 0.75 hollow (−0.2911), and Pt–Au 0.75 hollow (−0.2532) as near-optimal options within ±0.05 eV, depending on application tolerance.
  - Deprioritize Co/Ni–Cu for site-based tuning at 0.75; explore different compositions or pairs only if DB provides new entries.

How these results refine the hypothesis
- If Rh–Ag top approaches −0.27 ± 0.03, we will codify: for Rh hosts at 25% Ag, the top site is the corrective lever when hollow is too strong and bridge is inaccessible.
- If Pt–Au top weakens modestly beyond bridge, we strengthen the “small, monotonic site ladder” rule for Pt hosts.
- If Pd–Cu non-hollow sites are very weak, we generalize that Pd–coinage bridges/tops are coinage-rich ensembles that severely destabilize H, restricting viable operation to hollow.
