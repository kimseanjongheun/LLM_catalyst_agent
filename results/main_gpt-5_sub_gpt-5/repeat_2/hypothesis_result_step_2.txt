가설 수정 결과: Modified hypothesis based on the new simulation results

What held up
- Weak-binder primary + early strong-binder at 50/50 hollow is a robust route to the target. Ag–W (0.50/0.50, hollow) landed essentially on target (−0.2678 eV), and Cu–Mo (0.50/0.50, hollow) was slightly too strong (−0.2971 eV). This supports the idea that Mo/W in mixed hollow ensembles can effectively strengthen H binding on coinage primaries toward −0.27 eV.

What did not generalize
- “Mixed bridge at 0.75/0.25” is not broadly optimal for strong-binder primaries. Ni–Cu, Ni–Ag, and Co–Ag bridges were much too weak (−0.082 to +0.118 eV). Only Ru–Cu at the mixed bridge was near-optimal (−0.2967 eV). Thus, the magnitude of weakening induced by the weak-binder secondary (especially Ag) is larger than anticipated for Ni/Co.
- “Noble + Mo/W at 0.75/0.25 hollow” shows pair-dependent over/undershoot. Pt–Mo remained too weak (+0.0375 eV), whereas Pd–W and Rh–Mo overshot to too strong (−0.36 to −0.39 eV). Ir–W was intermediate (−0.1666 eV). The choice of Mo vs W and the site coordination must be used as tuning knobs; a single hollow prescription is not universally optimal.

Revised, testable hypothesis
1) Strong-binder primary diluted by weak-binder secondary (0.75/0.25) requires pair- and site-specific tuning:
- Ag as a diluent generally over-weakens H binding at mixed bridge sites for Ni/Co. Prefer Cu over Ag for Ni/Co primaries, and/or increase site coordination to mixed hollow to recover binding strength toward −0.27 eV.
- Very strong binders (e.g., Ru) paired with Cu can already be near-optimal at mixed bridge; modest weakening (e.g., using Ag) risks overshooting to too weak.

2) Noble/near-optimal primary + early strong-binder (0.75/0.25) is highly sensitive to the Mo/W choice and site coordination:
- W tends to strengthen more than Mo at mixed hollow sites (e.g., Pd–W, Rh–Mo both too strong), so swapping W→Mo or moving from hollow→bridge can correct over-strong binding.
- For under-strong cases (Pt–Mo), increase the early-metal strength (replace Mo with W) and/or increase the early-metal fraction (if permitted) or maintain hollow coordination to push toward −0.27 eV.
- Among nobles, Ir appears more readily tuned (Ir–W at −0.1666 eV); pairing Ir with Mo at hollow should strengthen further toward −0.27 eV.

3) Weak-binder primary requires early strong-binders and high coordination; late 3d partners are insufficient at 50/50:
- Coinage + Mo/W at 0.50/0.50 in mixed hollow ensembles is the most reliable path to the target. Ag–W is essentially optimal; Cu–Mo is slightly over-strong but close.
- Coinage + late 3d (Ni/Co/Fe) at 0.50/0.50 remains too weak (e.g., Cu–Ni −0.121 eV; Ag–Co +0.044 eV; Au–Fe +0.320 eV). Favor Mo/W over Ni/Co/Fe for coinage primaries.

Cross-cutting site trend update
- Mixed hollow ensembles generally increase H-binding strength relative to mixed bridge and thus are preferred when adsorption is too weak; mixed bridge can be used to weaken overly strong cases. A universal ordering (bridge ≈ best) is not supported; optimal site depends on the metal pair and whether one needs to strengthen or weaken H binding relative to the current value.

Knowledge accumulated from this iteration
- Strong-binder + Ag at 0.75/0.25 bridge over-weakens H adsorption for Ni/Co.
- Ru–Cu at 0.75/0.25 bridge is near-optimal (−0.2967 eV).
- Noble + Mo/W at 0.75/0.25 hollow spans under- to over-strong depending on the pair:
  - Pt–Mo: too weak (+0.0375 eV)
  - Pd–W, Rh–Mo: too strong (≤ −0.36 eV)
  - Ir–W: moderately weak (−0.1666 eV)
- Weak-binder primaries:
  - Ag–W (0.50/0.50, hollow) ≈ optimal (−0.2678 eV)
  - Cu–Mo (0.50/0.50, hollow) slightly too strong (−0.2971 eV)
  - Late 3d partners at 50/50 are ineffective in strengthening coinage to the target.
