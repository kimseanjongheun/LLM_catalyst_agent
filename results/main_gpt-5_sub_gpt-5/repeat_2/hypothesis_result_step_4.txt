What held up and how it refines our rules
- Hollow generally strengthens relative to bridge, but the magnitude is highly pair-dependent and can reverse or collapse for specific ensembles.
- 3d-in-coinage ensembles are extremely site-sensitive. For Ni/Co in Ag the hollow→bridge shift is much larger than in Cu:
  - Ni–Ag: +0.0725 (bridge) → −0.3703 eV (hollow), Δ ≈ −0.443 eV
  - Co–Ag: +0.1177 (bridge) → −0.3658 eV (hollow), Δ ≈ −0.484 eV
  This exceeds the ~0.25–0.30 eV shifts seen in Cu and highlights host-specific site amplification.
- Weak-binder primaries (coinage) still require early transition metals and high coordination to approach −0.27 eV, but composition responses are not monotonic across early metals:
  - Cu–Mo: 50/50 hollow −0.297 eV; at 75/25 (Cu-rich) hollow weakens drastically to −0.059 eV (diluting Mo weakens as expected).
  - Cu–W: 50/50 hollow −0.282 eV; at 75/25 hollow becomes much stronger, −0.543 eV (diluting W strengthens anomalously). Thus W vs Mo show opposite composition trends in Cu.

Key updates to the hypothesis
1) 3d primaries in coinage hosts at 0.75/0.25 are even more site-sensitive for Ag than for Cu:
- Bridge is far too weak (Ni–Ag +0.073 eV; Co–Ag +0.118 eV; Co–Cu −0.034 eV; Ni–Cu −0.082 eV).
- Hollow overshoots (Ni–Ag −0.370 eV; Co–Ag −0.366 eV; Ni–Cu −0.383 eV; Co–Cu −0.383 eV).
- Therefore, the optimum for 3d-in-coinage lies at intermediate composition and/or via careful site tuning, but “move to hollow” is not safe; the Ag host amplifies site sensitivity beyond Cu.

2) Mo vs W as the early partner (noble + early, hollow):
- Pd/Rh/Ir: W > Mo in strengthening holds, but the composition slope is steeper than anticipated:
  - Pd–W 50/50 hollow is on target (−0.277 eV).
  - Rh–W 50/50 hollow overshoots heavily (−0.461 eV).
  - Ir–W 50/50 hollow overshoots (−0.350 eV).
- Pt: W < Mo in strength holds; Pt–W 0.75/0.25 hollow remains weak (+0.121 eV). Increasing W to 50/50 should strengthen but may still under-shoot target; Mo at 50/50 would be preferred but appears absent in DB.

3) Coinage + early at hollow: composition response is early-metal specific
- W in Cu: dilution to 25% W can over-strengthen massively (Cu–W 75/25 hollow −0.543 eV), opposite to Mo.
- Mo in Cu: dilution weakens strongly (Cu–Mo 75/25 hollow −0.059 eV), consistent with expectation.
- Ag cases:
  - Ag–W 50/50 hollow is optimal (−0.268 eV).
  - Ag–Mo 50/50 hollow is very strong (−0.603 eV); unexpectedly, bridge does not weaken (−0.617 eV). Thus “use bridge to weaken” is not universal.

Cross-cutting site guidance (revised)
- Use hollow to strengthen when adsorption is too weak, but expect exceptionally large strengthening for 3d-in-Ag and substantial but smaller shifts for 3d-in-Cu. Overshoot risk is high.
- Bridge often weakens relative to hollow for many pairs but can fail or even slightly strengthen (Ag–Mo 50/50). Site effects must be mapped per pair; there is no universal bridge-weakens rule.
- If available, top sites should be considered only when both hollow and bridge overshoot (database permitting).

Revised, testable hypothesis (actionable)
- 3d-in-coinage systems at 0.75/0.25 are fundamentally site-oversensitive: bridge is too weak; hollow overshoots. The practical path to −0.27 eV is to lower 3d fraction toward 50/50 and use hollow (Co–Cu 50/50 hollow is a prime candidate).
- For Pd/Rh/Ir with W at hollow, W strengthens more than Mo and the strengthening slope with composition is steepest for Rh > Ir > Pd. Move to bridge to de-strengthen overshoot (if available), or reduce W fraction.
- For Pt with early metals, Mo > W in strengthening. Without Pt–Mo 50/50, Pt–W 50/50 hollow is worth mapping but may still underbind.
- For coinage + early, composition trends are element-specific:
  - With Mo: dilution strongly weakens (use 50/50 for Cu–Mo; reduce Mo for over-strong Ag–Mo).
  - With W: dilution can dramatically strengthen (Cu–W 75/25 overshoots); W is “safe” only near 50/50 for Ag/Cu. Avoid dilute-W in coinage if hollow is used.
